An attention lab notebook.

A slide-by-slide companion for the Advanced NLP course — every lecture, every tutorial page, and every past-year question, embedded verbatim and explained from first principles for someone who has never seen the material before.

6 lectures 240 slides 2 tutorials 3 PYQs public · MIT

§1What this is

The Advanced NLP course at IIIT Hyderabad is dense. Six lecture decks, two tutorial decks, and a growing tail of past-year questions cover everything from linear-algebra warm-ups to Flash Attention. If you're new to the material — which is exactly the case this guide was built for — the volume alone is intimidating.

This site is a reader's companion: every slide image appears exactly as it does in the deck, and every image is followed by a plain-language explanation. The explanations are the point. They aren't a paraphrase of the bullets on the slide — they're the paragraph the lecturer would say if you asked "wait, why does that matter?"

→ how to read this

Open a lecture page and scroll. Each "Polaroid" is one slide. Click any slide to see it full-size. The prose underneath is written so you can understand the slide without ever having seen the lecture. Callouts flag the aha-moments, the traps, and the math worth pausing on.

§3How the pages read

Each lecture and tutorial page follows the same shape. A short intro frames the deck. Then, one slide at a time:

the shape of each slide entry

The Polaroid. The exact slide image. Click to zoom.

What this shows. A one-line factual summary — for glancing.

The idea. Two to four sentences, plain language, the actual teaching.

Why it matters. Where this fits in the arc of the topic.

And, where useful, a math box or a "common misunderstanding" flag.

Slides that only make sense as a pair — a two-panel diagram, a before/after code snippet — are grouped into a strip and explained together. Otherwise it's one image, one paragraph.

✦ callout legend

Aha moments — a sticky-note that flags the key insight worth taking away.

⚠ common trap

A red-taped box for the mistake a first-time reader is most likely to make here.

⚙ mechanism

The math or the exact algorithm, boxed off so you can skim past on a first read and dive in on a second.

→ cross-reference

Where a concept was introduced earlier in the guide, so you can jump back if you missed it.
