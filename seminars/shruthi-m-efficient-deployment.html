§1What the seminar covers

Training LLMs and serving them are two very different engineering problems. Serving is where a model actually gets used, and its unit economics come from a small stack of well-known tricks:

  • Quantisation — running the model at INT8, INT4, or lower without noticeable quality loss.
  • KV-cache optimisation — paged, quantised, or shared caches that keep long-context inference within memory budgets.
  • Continuous batching — packing decode steps from many concurrent requests into a single GPU kernel launch instead of running them one at a time.
  • Speculative decoding — using a small draft model to propose several tokens at once, then verifying them cheaply with the big model.
  • Distillation and pruning — shrinking the model itself for the deployment target.

The seminar likely walks through several of these, with the goal of turning "we trained a nice model" into "we can serve it at a price users will pay".
